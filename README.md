# bribri_TTS


Modern text-to-speech (TTS) has advanced quickly for high-resource languages, but it remains out of reach for most Indigenous languages, which simply do not have the large aligned corpora these systems assume. We study Bribri, a tonal Chibchan language of Costa Rica with roughly 7,000 speakers, as a case of extreme low-resource TTS.
Using about 25 minutes of speech (449/56/56 clips) pooled from four native speakers, we compare three transfer-learning strategies on identical test sentences: fine-tuning MMS-ITS (VITS) from Cabécar, a close sister language; from Spanish, a distant high-resource one; and LoRA fine-tuning of Spark-TTS, an LLM-based model. By Mel Cepstral Distortion (MCD) the VITS systems scored best (Cabécar 3.06, Spanish 3.45 dB) against a 7.18 dB baseline, while Spark-TTS scored 6.44 dB. But a native speaker's Mean Opinion Score (MOS) reversed the ranking: Spark-TTS rated highest (2.75), tying Cabécar VITS (2.55) and beating both Spanish systems (2.10). Our central finding is that fine-tuning mattered far more than sister-language proximity. The metric disagreement supports our argument that the MCD gap is an artifact of Spark-TTS's lack of speaker conditioning, and that spectral distance alone cannot rank these architectures for tonal low-resource TTS.
